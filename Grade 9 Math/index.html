Distribution Type A

Symmetric / Bell-Shaped

Data is evenly balanced with no severe outliers.

Measure of Center:
MEAN (x̄) Average of all values
Measure of Variability (Spread):
STANDARD DEVIATION (s) Typical distance from mean
Why? Because there are no extreme outliers to pull or distort the calculations, mean and standard deviation use every single data point to give the most complete picture.
Distribution Type B

Skewed OR Outlier Present

Data has a long tail or extreme values that distort totals.

Measure of Center:
MEDIAN (Q2) Middle value (50th percentile)
Measure of Variability (Spread):
IQR (Interquartile Range) Spread of middle 50% (Q3 - Q1)
Why? Median and IQR are resistant to extreme values. A crazy outlier (like test score 0 or 100) will warp the mean, but will barely budge the median and IQR!

The 1.5 × IQR Outlier Boundary Rule

Standard IM1 Formula

In 9th grade Integrated Math 1, you can't just "guess" if a number looks too far away. You must prove it using the 1.5 × IQR rule:

1. Interquartile Range: IQR = Q3 - Q1
2. Lower Outlier Fence: Q1 - (1.5 × IQR)
3. Upper Outlier Fence: Q3 + (1.5 × IQR)

Any data point less than the Lower Fence or greater than the Upper Fence is an official outlier and must be plotted as an isolated dot on a modified box plot!
